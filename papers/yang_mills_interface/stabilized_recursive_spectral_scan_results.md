# Stabilized Recursive Spectral Scan Results

## Abstract

This document summarizes the first stabilized recursive spectral scan performed within the exploratory Yang–Mills Interface program of the Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) framework.

The objective is not claiming a Yang–Mills Mass Gap proof.

Instead, the purpose is documenting exploratory numerical evidence indicating that recursively stabilized operator systems may preserve positive low spectral sectors together with persistent spectral separation.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

---

# 1. Numerical Model

The numerical investigation studied recursively perturbed matrix operators of the form:

\[
H
=
H_0
+
V_{\mathrm{rec}}
+
\mu I
\]

where:

\[
H_0
=
2I
-
T_+
-
T_-
\]

represents a discrete Laplacian-type operator.

The recursive perturbation sector was defined through:

\[
V_{\mathrm{rec}}(x,\epsilon)
=
\epsilon \sin(4\pi x)
+
\frac{0.25}{1+10x^2}
\]

The stabilization parameter was chosen as:

\[
\mu = 1.25
\]

The matrix dimension was:

\[
N = 40
\]

---

# 2. Numerical Investigation Goal

The primary exploratory objectives were:

1. testing whether the lowest eigenvalue remains positive,

\[
\lambda_0 > 0
\]

2. and testing whether persistent spectral separation remains stable,

\[
\lambda_1-\lambda_0>0
\]

under recursively perturbed operator evolution.

No rigorous continuum Yang–Mills interpretation was assumed.

The stabilized recursive spectral scan produced:

\[
\min(\lambda_0)
=
0.040499
\]

and:

\[

=
0.042409
\]
