# Compactness Persistence Program

## Purpose

This document defines the exploratory compactness-persistence program within the Yang–Mills Interface framework of Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR).

The objective is not proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is investigating whether compactness and localization structures capable of supporting lower spectral persistence may remain stable under continuum refinement.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

---

# 1. Central Problem

The previous continuum-gap investigations identified the critical instability scenario:

\[
\lambda_1(N)-\lambda_0(N)\rightarrow0
\]

as:

\[
N\rightarrow\infty
\]

even if every individual operator:

\[
H_N
\]

possesses positive spectral separation.

The central analytical question is therefore whether compactness and localization structures remain sufficiently stable under refinement to suppress spectral escape and continuum gap collapse.

---

# 2. Spectral Escape Mechanism

A principal destabilizing mechanism is:

- excitation delocalization,
- spectral diffusion,
- loss of concentration,
- and escape of spectral mass toward increasingly diffuse continuum modes.

In such scenarios:

- eigenfunctions become progressively non-localized,
- compactness weakens,
- and lower spectral separation may collapse under refinement.

The framework investigates whether recursively admissible stabilization structures suppress such behavior.

---

# 3. Compactness Perspective

The framework investigates operator families:

\[
H_N
=
-\Delta_N
+
V_{\mathrm{conf},N}
+
V_{\mathrm{rec},N}
+
\mu I
\]

under increasing continuum refinement:

\[
N\rightarrow\infty
\]

The central exploratory hypothesis is:

Confinement-induced localization and recursive stabilization may preserve sufficient compactness structure to suppress destabilizing spectral escape.

---

# 4. Compact Resolvent Persistence

The framework investigates whether compact-resolvent structure remains sufficiently stable under refinement.

Potential stabilization mechanisms include:

- confinement-induced localization,
- bounded perturbative evolution,
- recursive compactness,
- and suppression of long-range excitation propagation.

Potential destabilizing mechanisms include:

- ultraviolet excitation proliferation,
- perturbative amplification,
- localization failure,
- and continuum spectral diffusion.

Potential destabilizing mechanisms include:

- excitation spreading,
