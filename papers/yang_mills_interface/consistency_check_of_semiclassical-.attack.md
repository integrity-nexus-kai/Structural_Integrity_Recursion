# Consistency Check of Semiclassical Attack

## Purpose

This document critically evaluates the internal consistency of the semiclassical counting attack proposed within the Yang–Mills Interface program.

The objective is not extending the framework.

Instead, the purpose is rigorously testing whether the semiclassical phase-space strategy is even conceptually compatible with the actual low-energy spectral-crowding problem.

This document represents the first explicit self-consistency audit of the semiclassical attack layer.

---

# 1. Central Issue

The previous framework proposed the heuristic relation:

\[
N_H(E)
\sim
\text{effective low-energy phase-space volume}.
\]

This motivated the idea that:

```text
confinement geometry
```

might suppress low-energy spectral proliferation through:

```text
phase-space reduction.
```

However this reasoning may be fundamentally problematic.

The present document evaluates these difficulties explicitly.

---

# 2. Core Conceptual Danger

The principal danger is:

```text
semiclassical asymptotics
primarily describe high-energy spectral behavior,
while the mass-gap problem
is fundamentally a low-energy problem.
```

This creates a potentially severe conceptual mismatch.

---

Standard Weyl asymptotics typically describe:

\[

\[

\sim

is fundamentally an asymptotic high-frequency statement.

The Yang–Mills Interface framework, however, is primarily concerned with:

```text
low-energy spectral separation.
```

Thus:

```text
the target regime may not be semiclassical at all.
```

# 4. Low-Energy Spectral Geometry May Behave Differently

Low-energy spectral geometry can be dominated by:

- tunneling structures,
- near-degenerate sectors,
- non-perturbative effects,
- global topology,
- or infinite-dimensional operator geometry.

These effects may be essentially invisible to naive semiclassical phase-space arguments.

Thus:

```text
low-energy crowding

```

# 5. Confinement May Not Reduce Low-Energy Density

Another critical issue is:

```text
confinement
does not automatically imply
low-energy spectral sparsity.
```

Confinement may:

- localize states,
- suppress spatial escape,
- or reduce high-energy transport,

while still allowing:

- extremely dense low-energy spectral sectors.

Thus:

```text
localization
≠
low-energy counting suppression.
```

This mirrors the earlier realization:

```text
compactness persistence
≠
spectral-gap persistence.
```

These phenomena may not admit semiclassical descriptions.
