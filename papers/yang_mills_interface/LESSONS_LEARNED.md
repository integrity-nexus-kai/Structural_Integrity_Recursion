# LESSONS_LEARNED

## Purpose

This document summarizes the principal conceptual and mathematical lessons learned during development of the Yang–Mills Interface program within Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR).

The objective of this file is:

- preserving critical insights,
- documenting conceptual corrections,
- preventing future drift,
- and identifying the actual mathematical core of the remaining problem.

This document should be treated as a canonical reflection and stabilization layer of the framework.

---

# 1. Most Important Overall Lesson

The most important insight of the entire program is now:

```text
The principal unresolved difficulty
is likely not compactness itself,
but asymptotic spectral crowding.
```

This defines the actual mathematical frontier of the framework.

One of the strongest outcomes of the framework is the realization that:

The most important correction of the program was recognizing that:

```text
compactness persistence
≠
spectral-gap persistence
```

The framework eventually revealed a fundamental distinction between:

```text
state geometry
```

and:

```text
spectral geometry.
```

This distinction became the decisive conceptual boundary of the program.

Another major lesson was:

```text

stability of spectral geometry.
```

\[

Thus:

```text
compact states
≠
stable spectral gaps.
```
