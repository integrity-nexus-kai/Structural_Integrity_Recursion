# Research Program Manifest

## Purpose

This document defines the scientific identity, structural objectives, methodological orientation, and long-term research direction of the Yang–Mills Interface framework within Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR).

The objective is not proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is explicitly defining the framework as an exploratory mathematical research program investigating whether stabilization-oriented continuum operator structures may support continuum-stable vacuum-relative spectral separation under admissible continuum refinement.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

No rigorous Yang–Mills Mass Gap proof is currently claimed.

---

# 1. Program Identity

The Yang–Mills Interface framework is an exploratory continuum operator research program.

The program investigates whether stabilization-oriented spectral structures may suppress destabilizing continuum gap-collapse mechanisms within progressively more physically meaningful non-linear operator systems.

The framework is fundamentally:

- operator-theoretic,
- spectral-analytic,
- continuum-oriented,
- stabilization-focused,
- and functional-analysis-based.

The framework investigates whether continuum-stable spectral persistence may emerge from combined structural stabilization mechanisms rather than from isolated single-component structures.

---

# 2. Central Research Objective

The central long-term objective of the program is investigating whether:

\[
\inf_N
\left(
\lambda_1(N)-\lambda_0(N)
\right)
>0
\]

may remain stable under admissible continuum refinement.

The framework investigates whether positive vacuum-relative spectral separation may persist through simultaneous stabilization of:

- lower spectral positivity,
- localization,
- compactness,
- ultraviolet behavior,
- perturbative boundedness,
- and vacuum-sector stability.

The central structural perspective of the program is:

Continuum-stable spectral persistence may require simultaneous stabilization of multiple interacting structural mechanisms.

The framework therefore investigates combined stabilization structures involving:

- compactness persistence,
- ultraviolet suppression,
- perturbative boundedness,

The framework hypothesizes that stable continuum spectral organization may emerge only through combined structural control.
