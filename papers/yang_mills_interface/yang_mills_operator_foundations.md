# Yang–Mills Operator Foundations

## Abstract

This document introduces the mathematical operator foundations relevant for exploratory Yang–Mills spectral investigations within the Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) framework.

The purpose is not claiming a Yang–Mills Mass Gap proof.

Instead, the objective is establishing the basic operator structures, gauge-field components, and spectral foundations required before any rigorous lower spectral analysis can be attempted.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

---

# 1. Introduction

Yang–Mills theory describes non-abelian gauge fields whose dynamics are governed by internal gauge symmetries.

Unlike abelian electromagnetic systems, non-abelian gauge fields possess self-interaction terms generated by the gauge algebra structure itself.

The fundamental Yang–Mills field strength tensor is:

\[
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu
-
\partial_\nu A_\mu
+
[A_\mu,A_\nu]
\]

where:

- \(A_\mu\) denotes the gauge field,
- and the commutator term introduces non-linear gauge self-interaction.

The non-linear operator structure generated by these interactions is central to all Yang–Mills spectral investigations.

---

# 2. Gauge Structure

Yang–Mills theories are constructed over non-abelian gauge groups such as:

\[
SU(2)
\quad
\text{or}
\quad
SU(3)
\]

The gauge field takes values in the associated Lie algebra:

\[
A_\mu
=
A_\mu^a T^a
\]

where:

- \(T^a\) are Lie algebra generators,
- and \(A_\mu^a\) are gauge components.

The non-commuting structure of the generators produces the non-linear interaction sector:

\[
[T^a,T^b]
=
f^{abc}T^c
\]

with structure constants:

\[
f^{abc}
\]

This non-abelian structure is fundamentally responsible for Yang–Mills self-interaction.

# 3. Yang–Mills Equations

The classical Yang–Mills equations are:

\[
D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=0
\]

where the covariant derivative is:

\[
D_\mu
=
\partial_\mu + [A_\mu,\cdot]
\]

These equations represent highly non-linear coupled operator systems.

Understanding the spectral properties of the associated quantum operators remains one of the central open problems in mathematical physics.

# 4. Quantum Operator Structure

A rigorous Yang–Mills Mass Gap investigation ultimately requires construction of a quantum Hamiltonian operator:

\[
H_{\mathrm{YM}}
\]

acting on an admissible quantum state space.

The central spectral question is whether:

\[
\inf \sigma(H_{\mathrm{YM}})>0
\]

holds above the vacuum sector.

The present framework does not yet construct a complete rigorous Yang–Mills Hamiltonian.

# 6. Spectral Perspective

The Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem is fundamentally a spectral problem.

The question is whether physically admissible excitations satisfy:

\[
E_1-E_0>0
\]

for the lowest non-trivial excitation sector.

This corresponds to the existence of a strictly positive lower spectral separation above the vacuum state.
