# Minimal Necessary Conditions

## Purpose

This document defines the exploratory minimal-necessary-conditions framework within the Yang–Mills Interface program of Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR).

The objective is not proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is identifying structural conditions that may be mathematically unavoidable for continuum-stable lower spectral persistence.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

No rigorous Yang–Mills Mass Gap proof is currently claimed.

---

# 1. Central Principle

A mathematically serious continuum-gap program must distinguish between:

- conditions that are merely useful,
- conditions that are heuristically plausible,
- and conditions that may be structurally necessary.

The objective of the present framework is identifying candidate structural requirements potentially unavoidable for stable vacuum-relative spectral separation under continuum refinement.

---

# 2. Central Continuum Question

The central analytical question is whether:

\[
\inf_N
\left(
\lambda_1(N)-\lambda_0(N)
\right)
>0
\]

may remain stable under admissible continuum scaling.

The framework investigates which structural properties may be necessary for suppressing destabilizing continuum gap collapse.

---

# 3. Necessary Condition A — Uniform Lower-Bound Control

## Candidate Requirement

There must exist:

\[
c_0>0
\]

independent of:

\[
N
\]

such that:

\[
\langle\psi,H_N\psi\rangle
\ge
c_0\|\psi\|^2
\]

for admissible states uniformly under refinement.

---

## Structural Motivation

Without uniform lower spectral control:

- arbitrarily low-energy modes may emerge,
- vacuum-relative spectral separation may collapse,
- and continuum-stable positivity becomes impossible.

---

# 4. Necessary Condition B — Localization Persistence

## Candidate Requirement

Low-energy spectral sectors must remain sufficiently localized under refinement.

Potential localization structures include:

- confinement concentration,
- bounded excitation propagation,
- and suppression of long-range spectral diffusion.

---

## Structural Motivation

Without localization persistence:

- low-energy states may delocalize,
- compactness may weaken,
- and spectral mass may escape toward diffuse continuum modes.

This may induce gap collapse.

---

# 5. Necessary Condition C — Compactness Persistence

## Candidate Requirement

---

# 6. Necessary Condition D — Ultraviolet Control

## Candidate Requirement

High-frequency excitation sectors must remain sufficiently controlled under refinement.

- bounded ultraviolet excitation growth,
- scale-stable spectral organization,

---

# 7. Necessary Condition E — Perturbative Boundedness

## Candidate Requirement

Recursive perturbation sectors must remain sufficiently bounded relative to reference operators uniformly under refinement.

Potential structures include:

\[
\|V_{\mathrm{rec},N}\psi\|
\le
a\|H_{0,N}\psi\|
+
b\|\psi\|
\]

with:

\[
a<1
\]

uniformly in:

\[
N
\]

---

## Structural Motivation

Without perturbative boundedness:

- self-adjointness stability may fail,
- lower spectral control may weaken,
- and continuum operator consistency may collapse.

---

The present framework suggests that continuum-stable lower spectral persistence may require simultaneous control of:

- lower spectral positivity,
- localization persistence,
- compactness stability,
- ultraviolet excitation growth,
- perturbative evolution,
- and vacuum-relative spectral organization.

Failure of any single structural component may destabilize continuum-gap persistence.
