This worksheet investigates whether asymptotic low-energy spectral counting functions may provide quantitative control of spectral crowding within the simplified continuum framework of the Yang–Mills Interface program.

The objective is not proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is introducing the first explicit quantitative spectral-growth strategy of the framework.

This document represents the transition from qualitative spectral-crowding analysis toward quantitative spectral-density geometry.

# 2. Spectral Counting Function

Define the spectral counting function:

\[
N_H(E)
=
\#\{\lambda_k\le E\}
\]

representing the number of spectral modes below energy level:

\[
E.
\]

This becomes the central quantitative object of the framework.

---

# 3. Why Counting Functions Matter

Spectral crowding corresponds to excessive accumulation of low-energy modes.

Thus:

```text
spectral-gap persistence
requires quantitative control
of low-energy spectral growth.
```

If:

\[
N_H(E)
\]

grows too rapidly near low energies, then:

- spectral separation weakens,
- low-energy density increases,
- and asymptotic gap collapse becomes likely.
