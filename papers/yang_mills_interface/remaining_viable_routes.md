# Remaining Viable Routes

## Purpose

This document evaluates which research directions within the Yang–Mills Interface framework remain plausibly viable after the consistency audit of the semiclassical counting attack.

The objective is not introducing new speculative structures.

Instead, the purpose is reducing the remaining research space through elimination of weak or conceptually inconsistent routes.

This document represents the first explicit viability-filter stage of the framework.

---

# 1. Current Situation

Previous development introduced:

- compactness structures,
- variational stabilization ideas,
- heat geometry,
- path geometry,
- entropy interpretations,
- counting-growth analysis,
- and semiclassical phase-space attacks.

The subsequent consistency audit then identified severe limitations of the semiclassical route.

The framework must therefore reassess:

```text
Which directions remain scientifically plausible?
```

---

# 2. Routes That Now Appear Weak

The following approaches now appear structurally insufficient as standalone strategies.

---

## A. Pure Compactness Arguments

The framework established:

```text
compactness persistence
≠
spectral-gap persistence.
```

Compactness controls:

- concentration,
- regularity,
- and state persistence,

but does not directly control:

- spectral density,
- spectral clustering,
- or low-energy mode proliferation.

Thus pure compactness arguments now appear insufficient.

---

## B. Naive Confinement Arguments

Confinement may suppress:

- spatial escape,
- path spreading,
- or high-energy transport.

However:

```text
confinement
does NOT automatically imply
low-energy spectral sparsity.
```

Dense low-energy spectral sectors may still survive.

Thus naive confinement arguments now appear insufficient.

---

## C. Naive Semiclassical Counting

The consistency audit identified a major conceptual problem:

```text
semiclassical asymptotics
primarily describe high-energy geometry,
while the mass-gap problem
is fundamentally low-energy.
```

Thus semiclassical counting may fail to probe the genuinely dangerous spectral sectors.

---

## D. Simple Heat-Trace Stability

Heat traces may remain controlled while:

- extremely fine low-energy spectral clustering persists,
- and asymptotic gap collapse still occurs.

Thus:

```text
heat stability
≠
spectral-gap persistence.
```

---

# 3. Routes That May Still Remain Viable

The following directions may still contain meaningful research potential.

---

## A. Operator Geometry

The framework increasingly suggests that the true problem is:

```text
operator-level spectral organization.
```

Potentially relevant structures may include:

- resolvent geometry,
- spectral projection structure,
- operator convergence,
- and asymptotic spectral organization.

This currently appears to remain one of the strongest surviving directions.

---

## B. Low-Energy Resolvent Structure

The framework increasingly points toward:

```text
low-energy operator behavior
```

rather than global phase-space geometry.

Potentially:

\[
(H-zI)^{-1}
\]

near low-energy regimes may encode critical spectral organization information.

This direction remains completely undeveloped.

---

## C. Infinite-Dimensional Spectral Organization

The framework increasingly recognizes that the core problem may involve:

```text
continuous generation
of new low-energy sectors
under refinement.
```

This suggests that the decisive mechanisms may be genuinely infinite-dimensional and non-semiclassical.

---

## D. Non-Perturbative Spectral Geometry

The framework increasingly suggests that:

```text
the dangerous low-energy sectors
may be fundamentally non-perturbative.
```

Potentially relevant structures may involve:

- collective spectral organization,
- asymptotic operator topology,
- or nonlocal spectral interactions.

No such theory currently exists within the framework.

---

# 4. Most Important Surviving Insight

The strongest surviving insight is now:

```text
The core problem
likely concerns
low-energy operator organization,
not classical phase-space geometry.
```

This is now the dominant conceptual direction of the framework.

The framework has now eliminated several major simplifications.

```text
gap persistence
```

```text

spectral sparsity
```

This is the scientifically correct current interpretation.

```text
Can asymptotic low-energy operator organization
be controlled
```

This now appears to be the sharpest surviving formulation of the framework.

Potential future directions may involve:

None currently exist rigorously within the framework.

---

# 9. Important Scientific Limitation

The present framework currently does NOT establish:

- low-energy spectral sparsity,
- operator-level spectral stabilization,
- asymptotic spectral separation,
- spectral-gap persistence,
- continuum spectral stability,
- or Yang–Mills mass-gap results.

The present document only isolates which routes currently remain scientifically plausible.

---

# 10. Current Scientific Position

The scientifically correct current position is:

- several earlier heuristic routes now appear structurally insufficient,
- the surviving directions increasingly point toward genuinely low-energy operator geometry,
- and the core unresolved problem likely lies beyond compactness and semiclassical phase-space analysis.

However:

```text
no rigorous low-energy operator-control mechanism
currently exists within the framework.
```
