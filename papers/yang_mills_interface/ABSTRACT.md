# ABSTRACT

## Exploratory Asymptotic Compactness Framework for Low-Energy Spectral Persistence

We develop an exploratory asymptotic compactness framework for investigating whether confinement-induced concentration mechanisms may stabilize low-energy spectral structure under continuum refinement in simplified continuum operator settings related to Yang–Mills-type spectral problems.

The framework studies asymptotic operator families of the form

\[
H_N=-\Delta+V_N
\]

under bounded low-energy spectral assumptions and confinement-growth conditions.

The central analytical program investigates the following compactness chain:

```text
bounded energy
        ↓
confinement growth
        ↓
tightness
        ↓
local H¹-bounds
        ↓
Rellich compactness
        ↓
strong local convergence
        ↓
global mass persistence
        ↓
nontrivial limit states
```

The framework proposes that confinement-induced tightness may suppress asymptotic spectral-mass escape and preserve nontrivial low-energy asymptotic structures.

Building on this compactness architecture, the program additionally investigates exploratory variational and operator-theoretic stabilization mechanisms, including:

- quadratic-form stabilization,
- Γ-type variational persistence,
- and possible operator-convergence strategies.

However, the framework explicitly identifies a major unresolved obstruction separating compactness persistence from genuine spectral-gap persistence.

The central unresolved instability mechanism is:

```text
asymptotic spectral crowding
```

where infinitely many low-energy modes may compress together asymptotically, potentially causing:

\[
\lambda_1(N)-\lambda_0(N)\rightarrow0
\]

even while compactness structures remain stable.

The framework therefore concludes that compactness persistence alone does NOT imply spectral-gap persistence.

At the current stage, the program establishes only an exploratory asymptotic compactness and spectral-geometry research framework.

No rigorous operator convergence, spectral-gap persistence, or Yang–Mills mass-gap proof currently exists within the framework.

The central open problem identified by the program is whether asymptotic spectral density can be controlled strongly enough to suppress spectral crowding under continuum refinement.
