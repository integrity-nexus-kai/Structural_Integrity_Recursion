# Yang–Mills Variational Operator

## Abstract

This document investigates exploratory variational structures potentially relevant for future Yang–Mills spectral analysis within the Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) framework.

The objective is not claiming a Yang–Mills Mass Gap proof.

Instead, the purpose is identifying variational lower-bound structures, admissibility conditions, and recursive spectral stabilization mechanisms that may eventually become mathematically relevant for non-linear gauge operator systems.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

---

# 1. Introduction

The Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem is fundamentally a spectral and variational problem associated with non-abelian quantum gauge operators.

One central question is whether admissible quantum excitation sectors satisfy:

\[
E_1-E_0>0
\]

above the vacuum state.

The present work investigates whether recursive variational structures developed within SIR may eventually provide useful mathematical tools for studying lower spectral organization in non-linear gauge systems.

# 2. Yang–Mills Energy Structure

The classical Yang–Mills action is based on the field strength tensor:

\[
F_{\mu\nu}
=
\partial_\mu A_\nu
-
\partial_\nu A_\mu
+
[A_\mu,A_\nu]
\]

The corresponding Yang–Mills energy structure is formally associated with:

\[
\mathcal{E}[A]
=
\int
|F_{\mu\nu}|^2
\, d^4x
\]

The non-linear commutator contribution:

\[
[A_\mu,A_\nu]
\]

introduces highly non-trivial interaction dynamics.

# 3. Variational Perspective

The Yang–Mills spectral problem may be viewed variationally through admissible perturbative energy sectors.

One central variational question is whether lower spectral estimates of the form:

\[

\geq

\]

may exist for admissible non-trivial gauge sectors.

The existence of such lower bounds would be related to stable positive spectral separation.

The present framework does not establish such a proof.
