# Model Regime Testing

## Purpose

This document investigates whether the asymptotic stabilization condition

\[
p-2q>0
\]

can plausibly arise within analytically reasonable recursive refinement regimes.

The objective is NOT proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is testing whether the asymptotic balance identified in the refinement-stability framework admits mathematically plausible realization scenarios.

This document represents the first explicit analytical-regime testing phase of the refinement-stability program.

---

# 1. Motivation

Previous development produced the first explicit asymptotic stabilization condition:

\[
\eta_N^{-2}\varepsilon_N\to0.
\]

Under polynomial asymptotics:

\[
\varepsilon_N\sim N^{-p},
\]

and:

\[
\eta_N\sim N^{-q},
\]

this becomes:

\[
p-2q>0.
\]

The framework now reaches a critical analytical question:

```text
Do mathematically plausible refinement regimes
exist
for which:
p-2q>0 ?
```

The present document investigates this problem directly.

---

# 2. Central Regime-Testing Principle

The framework now adopts the following principle:

```text
an asymptotic stabilization condition
is only meaningful
if analytically plausible regimes
can realize it.
```

This principle becomes central for all future theorem development.

---

# 3. Test Regime A

# Uniform Spectral Spacing

Assume:

\[
q=0.
\]

Thus:

\[
\eta_N\sim 1.
\]

---

## Consequence

Then:

\[
p-2q=p>0.
\]

Thus any perturbation decay:

\[
p>0
\]

formally stabilizes the resolvent estimate.

---

## Structural Interpretation

No asymptotic spectral crowding occurs.

---

## Scientific Status

Analytically idealized.

Potentially unrealistic in highly recursive systems.

---

# 4. Test Regime B

# Weak Polynomial Crowding

Assume:

\[
0<q<\frac12.
\]

---

## Consequence

Stabilization requires:

\[
p>2q.
\]

Example:

\[
q=\frac14
\]

requires:

\[
p>\frac12.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Moderate spacing collapse remains admissible if perturbations decay sufficiently rapidly.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically realistic.

---

# 5. Test Regime C

# Strong Spectral Condensation

Assume:

\[
q>\frac12.
\]

---

## Consequence

Very rapid perturbation decay becomes necessary.

Example:

\[
q=1
\]

requires:

\[
p>2.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Severe spectral crowding amplifies resolvent instability strongly.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically unstable.

---

# 6. Test Regime D

# Exponential Perturbation Decay

Assume:

\[
\varepsilon_N
\sim
e^{-N}.
\]

---

## Consequence

Exponential decay dominates all polynomial spacing collapse:

\[
N^{2q}e^{-N}\to0.
\]

Thus:

\[
\eta_N^{-2}\varepsilon_N\to0
\]

for all polynomial \(q\).

---

## Structural Interpretation

Extremely rapid perturbative stabilization suppresses resolvent amplification completely.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically powerful,
but structurally restrictive.

---

# 7. Test Regime E

# Logarithmic Spacing Collapse

Assume:

\[
\eta_N
\sim
(\log N)^{-\beta}.
\]

---

## Consequence

Then:

\[
\eta_N^{-2}
\sim
(\log N)^{2\beta}.
\]

Polynomial perturbation decay dominates logarithmic spacing collapse easily.

---

## Structural Interpretation

Weak spectral crowding remains analytically manageable.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically favorable.

---

# 8. Test Regime F

# Random-Matrix-Inspired Spacing Behavior

Potentially:

low-energy spacing behaves statistically rather than deterministically.

---

## Candidate Structure

Spacing collapse occurs probabilistically with suppressed extreme crowding events.

---

## Consequence

Average resolvent amplification may remain bounded despite local irregularity.

---

## Structural Interpretation

Weak statistical rigidity stabilizes asymptotic projection behavior.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially mathematically interesting,
but currently highly speculative.

---

# 9. Test Regime G

# Subsequence-Only Stabilization

Potentially:

full asymptotic stabilization fails globally but survives along admissible subsequences.

---

## Candidate Structure

There exists:

\[
N_k
\]

such that:

\[
N_k^{2q-p}\to0.
\]

---

## Consequence

Weak asymptotic stabilization survives partially.

---

## Structural Interpretation

Admissible refinement stabilization may be subsequential rather than global.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially mathematically realistic.
