# Resolvent Growth Regimes

## Purpose

This document investigates which asymptotic resolvent-growth regimes remain compatible with asymptotic stabilization under recursive perturbative refinement.

The objective is NOT proving the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the purpose is classifying admissible growth behavior of local resolvent norms within the key stabilization lemma framework.

This document represents the first explicit resolvent-growth classification phase of the refinement-stability program.

---

# 1. Motivation

Previous development isolated the first technical key lemma candidate:

\[
\|R_{N+1}(z)-R_N(z)\|
\le
C_{N+1}C_N\varepsilon_N.
\]

The entire stabilization mechanism now depends on the asymptotic competition between:

- perturbation decay,
and:
- resolvent-growth amplification.

The framework therefore reaches its first direct asymptotic-growth problem:

```text
Which resolvent-growth regimes
remain compatible
with asymptotic stabilization?
```

The present document investigates this question directly.

---

# 2. Central Growth Principle

The framework now adopts the following principle:

```text
stabilization survives
only when resolvent growth
remains asymptotically dominated
by perturbative decay.
```

This principle becomes central for all future theorem construction.

---

# 3. Growth Regime A

# Uniformly Bounded Resolvent Growth

Assume:

\[
C_N
\le
C.
\]

uniformly.

---

## Consequence

Then:

\[
C_{N+1}C_N\varepsilon_N
\lesssim
\varepsilon_N
\to0.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Perturbative decay completely dominates resolvent amplification.

---

## Possible Consequences

Potentially:

- asymptotic resolvent stabilization,
- projection continuity,
- and dominant regular refinement flow.

---

## Scientific Status

Analytically idealized.

---

# 4. Growth Regime B

# Polynomial Resolvent Growth

Assume:

\[
C_N
\sim
N^\alpha.
\]

---

## Consequence

Then:

\[
C_{N+1}C_N\varepsilon_N
\sim
N^{2\alpha}\varepsilon_N.
\]

If:

\[
\varepsilon_N
\sim
N^{-p},
\]

stabilization requires:

\[
2\alpha-p<0.
\]

Equivalently:

\[
p>2\alpha.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Polynomial resolvent amplification remains admissible if perturbative decay dominates sufficiently strongly.

---

## Possible Consequences

Potentially:

- weak asymptotic stabilization,
- almost-everywhere projection continuity,
- and bounded instability density.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically realistic.

---

# 5. Growth Regime C

# Logarithmic Resolvent Growth

Assume:

\[
C_N
\sim
(\log N)^\alpha.
\]

---

## Consequence

Then:

\[
C_{N+1}C_N\varepsilon_N
\sim
(\log N)^{2\alpha}\varepsilon_N.
\]

Polynomial perturbation decay dominates logarithmic growth easily.

---

## Structural Interpretation

Weak logarithmic amplification remains asymptotically harmless.

---

## Possible Consequences

Potentially:

- dominant stabilization,
- weak long-range smoothing,
- and almost-everywhere regularity.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically favorable.

---

# 6. Growth Regime D

# Subcritical Admissible Growth

Assume directly:

\[
C_N^2\varepsilon_N
\to0.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Resolvent amplification remains asymptotically weaker than perturbative damping.

---

## Possible Consequences

Potentially:

\[
\|R_{N+1}(z)-R_N(z)\|
\to0.
\]

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially analytically central.

---

# 7. Growth Regime E

\[

---

## Structural Interpretation

## Possible Consequences

# 8. Growth Regime F

# Supercritical Divergence

Assume:

\[
C_N^2\varepsilon_N
\to\infty.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Resolvent amplification overwhelms perturbative decay.

---

## Possible Consequences

---

## Scientific Status

# 9. Growth Regime G

# Almost-Everywhere Bounded Subsequences

Potentially:

full resolvent stabilization fails globally but survives on asymptotically dominant regular subsequences.

---

## Candidate Structure

Potentially:

\[
C_N^2\varepsilon_N
\to0
\]

for:

\[
N\in\mathcal R,
\]

with:

\[
\frac{|\mathcal R\cap[1,N]|}{N}
\to1.
\]

---

## Structural Interpretation

Regular refinement scales dominate pathological growth asymptotically.

---

## Possible Consequences

Potentially:

- almost-everywhere stabilization,
- sparse instability persistence,
- and dominant projection continuity.

---

## Scientific Status

Potentially mathematically realistic.
