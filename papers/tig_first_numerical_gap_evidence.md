# TIG First Numerical Gap Evidence

## Abstract

This paper presents the first numerical lower spectral investigation of the recursive perturbative operator associated with Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The analysis studies the lowest perturbative eigenvalue sector of the effective recursive radial operator:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}}
=
-\frac{d^2}{dx^2}
+
V_{\mathrm{rec}}(x,\beta)
\]

with recursive potential:

\[
V_{\mathrm{rec}}(x,\beta)
=
-\frac{\alpha}{x^2}
+
\lambda(\beta-\beta_c)^2
\]

The numerical investigation indicates the existence of positive lower spectral sectors within Hardy-stable recursive parameter regimes.

The framework remains exploratory and structural.

No physical Yang–Mills mass-gap claim is currently made.

The recursive spectral framework of TIG investigates whether recursively admissible geometric structures can dynamically generate stable lower perturbative spectral sectors.

\[

\[

# 5. Numerical Ground-State Scan

The TIG radial operator was discretized using finite-difference spectral methods.

The numerical investigation tracked the lowest perturbative eigenvalue:

\[
\omega_0(\beta)
\]

throughout the recursive parameter scan.

The resulting numerical lower spectral sector satisfied:

\[
\min(\omega_0^2)
=
0.00557819
\]

and:

\[
\max(\omega_0^2)
=
0.06807661
\]

throughout the investigated parameter region.
