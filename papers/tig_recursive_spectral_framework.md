# TIG Recursive Spectral Framework

## Abstract

This document presents the recursive spectral framework underlying Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The framework investigates whether recursively constrained critical geometries can generate stable spectral structures through non-linear geometric self-organization.

The objective is not to claim a solution to the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem or a complete quantum gravity formalism.

The objective is to establish a mathematically analyzable recursive geometric framework connecting:

- critical transition behavior,
- recursive admissibility,
- perturbative stability,
- topological consistency,
- and emergent spectral organization.

The framework remains exploratory and structural.

---

# 1. Introduction

Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG) investigates recursively constrained geometric systems characterized by critical transition structures and admissibility-preserving evolution.

The central structural relation is:

\[
x^3 - x^2 + \beta^3 = 0
\]

with:

\[
x := \frac{r_H}{2M}
\]

and:

\[
\beta := \frac{r_c}{2M}
\]

The relation defines a recursive geometric transition structure exhibiting bifurcation behavior and critical stability sectors.

The primary scientific question concerns whether recursively constrained geometries can generate stable lower-bounded perturbative spectra.

---

# 2. Recursive Geometric Structure

TIG treats admissible geometries as recursively constrained structural sectors.

Not all formally possible configurations are considered admissible.

Recursive geometric integrity imposes constraints on:

- continuity,
- perturbative boundedness,
- topological consistency,
- and recursive structural stability.

Only recursively self-consistent geometries remain admissible under recursive evolution.

# 3. Perturbative Framework

Perturbative fluctuations are introduced through:

\[
g_{\mu\nu}
\rightarrow
g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}
\]

where:

- \(g_{\mu\nu}\) denotes admissible TIG background geometries,
- and \(h_{\mu\nu}\) represents recursively admissible perturbative fluctuations.

The objective is to determine whether perturbative sectors remain recursively bounded.

---

# 4. Recursive Stability Operator

Perturbative evolution is investigated through the recursive stability operator:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}} h = \omega^2 h
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}}\) denotes the recursive geometric stability operator,
- \(h\) represents admissible fluctuation modes,
- and \(\omega^2\) defines the associated spectral eigenstructure.

The framework investigates whether recursively admissible operator sectors produce stable bounded spectral domains.

- spectral admissibility,

Recursive topological admissibility restricts geometric transitions to structurally self-consistent sectors.

The admissibility framework therefore acts as a recursive integrity filter.

A central research objective is determining whether TIG admits recursively stable lower-bounded perturbative spectra:

\[

\]

The existence of such spectral lower bounds may indicate emergent gap-like behavior generated through recursive geometric constraints.

No physical mass-gap claim is currently made.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.
