# TIG Mathematical Core Framework

## Abstract

This document presents the mathematical core framework underlying Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The framework investigates whether recursively constrained critical geometries can generate stable perturbative spectral structures through recursive geometric self-organization.

The objective is not to claim a complete quantum gravity theory or a solution to the Yang–Mills Mass Gap problem.

Instead, the framework establishes a mathematically analyzable recursive geometric structure connecting:

- recursive spectral operators,
- variational dynamics,
- perturbative stability,
- topological admissibility,
- recursive energy structures,
- and emergent lower-bounded spectral organization.

The framework remains exploratory and mathematically structural.

---

# 1. Introduction

Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG) investigates recursively constrained critical geometries exhibiting admissibility-preserving perturbative evolution.

The framework is centered around the recursive transition relation:

\[
x^3 - x^2 + \beta^3 = 0
\]

with:

\[
x := \frac{r_H}{2M}
\]

and:

\[
\beta := \frac{r_c}{2M}
\]

The recursive transition structure exhibits bifurcation behavior and critical spectral sensitivity near:

\[
\beta \rightarrow \beta_c
\]

where:

\[
\beta_c
=
\left(
\frac{4}{27}
\right)^{1/3}
\]

The primary objective of TIG is investigating whether recursively constrained geometries generate stable lower-bounded perturbative spectral sectors.

\[

- and \(V_{\mathrm{rec}}(x,\beta)\) represents the recursive geometric potential.

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}} h
=
\omega^2 h
\]

where:

- \(h\) denotes recursively admissible perturbative modes,
- and \(\omega^2\) defines the associated spectral eigenstructure.
