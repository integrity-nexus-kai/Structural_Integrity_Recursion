## Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR)

# Purpose

This document defines the canonical conceptual and mathematical core of the Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) framework.

Its purpose is to preserve:

- semantic consistency,
- recursive admissibility,
- mathematical stability,
- and controlled abstraction boundaries.

Only structures considered sufficiently stabilized should be treated as canonical.

---

# Canonical Principle

SIR proceeds from the assumption that recursive systems require admissibility conditions in order to remain structurally stable.

The framework therefore investigates whether recursive processes can remain:

- bounded,
- semantically coherent,
- structurally admissible,
- and recursively stabilizable.

# Core Canonical Concepts

## 1. Recursive Admissibility

Recursive systems must satisfy internal consistency constraints in order to avoid uncontrolled structural divergence.

Admissibility is therefore treated as a structural condition rather than a purely computational property.

---

## 2. Structural Integrity

Structural integrity refers to the preservation of coherent recursive organization under increasing abstraction and recursive depth.

The framework studies whether recursive systems can preserve:

- semantic continuity,
- bounded recursive evolution,
- and stable abstraction mappings.

---

## 3. Recursive Stabilization

Recursive stabilization refers to the emergence or preservation of bounded recursive behavior under iterative structural transformation.

The framework does not assume unlimited recursive stability.

Instead, recursive admissibility may fail beyond certain structural thresholds.

---

## 4. Semantic Consistency

SIR treats semantic consistency as a structural property of recursive systems.

The framework investigates whether abstraction layers remain recursively compatible across transformations.

---

## 5. Topology-Aware Recursion

Recursive systems may possess structural transition boundaries analogous to topology-sensitive phase transitions.

The framework therefore studies recursive organization with explicit attention to:

- structural connectivity,
- admissibility boundaries,
- recursive transition behavior,
- and abstraction-layer stability.
