# TIG Admissible State Space

## Purpose

This document defines the admissible recursive state-space structures associated with Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The objective is to establish mathematically consistent classes of geometric states compatible with recursive structural integrity conditions.

The framework focuses on recursively admissible perturbative and geometric configurations rather than unrestricted solution spaces.

---

# Conceptual Foundation

TIG is based on the assumption that not all formally possible geometric configurations are structurally admissible.

Recursive integrity constraints restrict the physically meaningful state space to geometries satisfying:

- structural continuity,
- recursive consistency,
- bounded perturbative evolution,
- and admissibility-preserving transition behavior.

The admissible state space therefore represents the recursively stable sector of the TIG framework.

---

# Structural Transition Basis

The admissible state space is constructed around the TIG structural relation:

\[
x^3 - x^2 + \beta^3 = 0
\]

with:

\[
x := \frac{r_H}{2M}
\]

and:

\[
\beta := \frac{r_c}{2M}
\]

Only geometries satisfying recursive admissibility conditions are considered valid elements of the state space.

---

# Admissible States

An admissible TIG state is defined as a geometric configuration satisfying:

- recursive structural consistency,
- bounded curvature behavior,
- perturbative stability,
- and continuity under recursive evolution.

Admissible states must remain structurally coherent under admissible fluctuations.

---

# Recursive Constraints

The admissible state space is constrained by:

- topological admissibility,
- recursive manifold consistency,
- operator-domain compatibility,
- and integrity-preserving transition conditions.

Configurations violating recursive stability conditions are excluded from the admissible sector.

Perturbative sectors are introduced through fluctuations:

\[

\rightarrow
g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}
\]

The admissible state space contains only perturbative configurations satisfying:

\[
\|h_{\mu\nu}\| < \infty
\]

under recursive geometric evolution.

The objective is to identify recursively stable perturbative sectors.
