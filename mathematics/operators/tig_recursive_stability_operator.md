# TIG Recursive Stability Operator

## Purpose

This document introduces the recursive stability operator framework used to investigate perturbative stability and spectral behavior within Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The purpose is not to define a complete quantum field operator formalism.

The purpose is to establish a mathematically analyzable recursive stability structure associated with critical geometric transitions.

---

# Conceptual Motivation

TIG is based on recursively constrained geometric structures exhibiting bifurcation behavior and critical transition regimes.

The central structural relation:

\[
x^3 - x^2 + \beta^3 = 0
\]

defines admissible geometric transition sectors.

To investigate the stability of these sectors, perturbative fluctuations must be analyzed through an effective recursive stability operator.

---

# Perturbative Framework

Admissible perturbations are introduced as:

\[
g_{\mu\nu}
\rightarrow
g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}
\]

where:

- \(g_{\mu\nu}\) denotes the admissible TIG background geometry,
- and \(h_{\mu\nu}\) represents small recursive perturbative fluctuations.

The objective is to determine whether perturbations remain recursively bounded under geometric evolution.

---

# Recursive Stability Operator

The perturbative evolution is formally represented through a recursive stability operator:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}} h = \omega^2 h
\]

where:

- \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}}\) denotes the recursive stability operator,
- \(h\) represents admissible fluctuation modes,
- and \(\omega^2\) defines the associated spectral eigenstructure.

The operator is interpreted as an effective geometric consistency operator governing recursive perturbative admissibility.

---

# Stability Conditions

A recursively admissible TIG background is expected to satisfy:

- bounded perturbative evolution,
- absence of divergent recursive amplification,
- spectral consistency,
- and geometric continuity under recursive fluctuations.

The investigation focuses on whether the operator spectrum remains:

\[
\omega^2 \geq 0
\]

under admissible structural conditions.

---

# Critical Regime Analysis

Special attention is given to the near-critical regime:

\[
\beta \rightarrow \beta_c
\]

where:

\[
\beta_c = \left(\frac{4}{27}\right)^{1/3}
\]

In this region recursive transition dynamics become structurally sensitive.

Potential phenomena include:

- mode localization,
- recursive stabilization sectors,
- spectral clustering,
- and emergent lower spectral bounds.

# Spectral Gap Investigation

A central research objective is determining whether the recursive operator admits:

\[
\omega_{\min} > 0
\]

for admissible perturbative sectors.

If such a lower spectral bound exists, TIG may exhibit emergent gap-like behavior generated through recursive geometric constraints.

No physical mass-gap claim is currently made.

The present framework remains exploratory and structural.
