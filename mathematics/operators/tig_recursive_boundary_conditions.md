# TIG Recursive Boundary Conditions

## Purpose

This document defines the recursive boundary conditions associated with perturbative and geometric structures in Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG).

The objective is to establish mathematically admissible boundary sectors required for recursive spectral stability and geometric consistency.

The recursive boundary framework determines which perturbative configurations remain admissible under recursive geometric evolution.

---

# Conceptual Foundation

The recursive stability operator:

\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TIG}}
\]

is only well-defined once admissible boundary conditions are specified.

Boundary structures determine:

- spectral admissibility,
- perturbative boundedness,
- geometric continuity,
- and recursive stability behavior.

The recursive boundary framework therefore acts as a structural consistency layer for the TIG spectral system.

---

# Structural Basis

The TIG framework is based on the recursive transition relation:

\[
x^3 - x^2 + \beta^3 = 0
\]

with:

\[
x := \frac{r_H}{2M}
\]

and:

\[
\beta := \frac{r_c}{2M}
\]

The admissibility of perturbative sectors depends on the recursive geometric behavior near critical transition regions.

# Asymptotic Boundary Conditions

For asymptotically admissible sectors:

\[
r \rightarrow \infty
\]

perturbative fluctuations are required to remain recursively bounded.

Admissible asymptotic conditions include:

- finite perturbative norm,
- spectral boundedness,
- recursive continuity,
- and non-divergent geometric evolution.

Configurations exhibiting unbounded recursive amplification are excluded from the admissible sector.

---

# Perturbative Boundary Conditions

Perturbative fluctuations are introduced through:

\[
g_{\mu\nu}
\rightarrow
g_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}
\]

Admissible perturbations must satisfy:

\[
\|h_{\mu\nu}\| < \infty
\]

throughout recursive evolution.

The recursive boundary framework restricts perturbations to geometrically stable sectors.
