# RECURSIVE TRANSITION MECHANICS

## Purpose

This document defines recursive transition dynamics within admissibility-preserving recursive systems.

The objective is to formalize how recursive systems:

* stabilize,
* destabilize,
* bifurcate,
* collapse,
* or transition between admissibility regimes.

---

# 1. Recursive Transition Principle

Recursive systems evolve through recursive state transitions.

A transition occurs when recursive evolution changes admissibility structure.

Transitions may be:

* stable,
* metastable,
* critical,
* bifurcating,
* or divergent.

---

# 2. Transition Operator

Let:

T : S_n → S_{n+1}

be a recursive transition operator.

The recursive admissibility evolution is:

A(S_{n+1}) = F(A(S_n), T_n)

where:

* A defines admissibility,
* and F defines recursive admissibility propagation.

---

# 3. Stable Recursive Transitions

A transition is stable if:

A(S_n) ≥ 0

for all recursive depth n.

Stable transitions preserve recursive integrity.

Such systems remain:

* bounded,
* recursively coherent,
* and structurally admissible.

---

# 4. Metastable Recursive States

Metastable recursive states exist near critical admissibility boundaries.

Properties include:

* temporary stability,
* sensitivity to perturbation,
* delayed transition behavior,
* and recursive tension accumulation.

Metastability represents structurally unstable equilibrium.

---

# 5. Recursive Bifurcation

A recursive bifurcation occurs when a recursive trajectory splits into multiple admissible or partially admissible branches.

Bifurcation regions occur near:

A(S_n) → 0

Recursive bifurcation may produce:

* topology transition,
* recursive divergence,
* attractor splitting,
* or collapse propagation.

---

# 6. Recursive Collapse

Recursive collapse occurs when:

A(S_n) < 0

Collapse indicates inadmissible recursive evolution.

Collapse dynamics may include:

* integrity drift,
* uncontrolled recursion,
* recursive amplification,
* recursive fragmentation,
* or structural incoherence.

---

# 7. Transition Surfaces

A transition surface defines the boundary between admissible and inadmissible recursive evolution.

Formally:

A(S_n) = 0

Transition surfaces define:

* critical recursive geometry,
* recursive instability thresholds,
* and admissibility phase boundaries.

# 9. Recursive Attractors

Recursive attractors describe recursively stable admissibility regions toward which recursive trajectories converge.

Attractors may be:

* stable,
* metastable,
* cyclic,
* or collapsing.
