# GLOBAL GLOSSARY — UNIFIED PRIMITIVES

Version 1.0

---

# PURPOSE

This document defines the unified primitive terminology shared across:

- TIG (Topological Integrity Gravity)
- SIR (Structural Integrity Recursion)
- SGI (Sovereign Guardian Integrity)

The purpose is semantic stabilization.

All repositories must preserve consistency with these definitions.

This glossary is not implementation-specific.

It defines structural primitives.

---

# CORE PRINCIPLE

A primitive is a minimal irreducible structural concept required for recursive integrity-preserving systems.

Primitives must:

- remain internally consistent,
- preserve admissibility,
- support recursive propagation,
- and avoid semantic contradiction across layers.

---

# INTEGRITY

Integrity is the preservation of structurally admissible state evolution under recursive transformation.

Integrity is not morality.

Integrity is structural consistency preservation.

---

# ADMISSIBILITY

Admissibility describes whether a state, transition, operator, or evolution path satisfies the minimal structural consistency conditions of the system.

Non-admissible states may formally exist mathematically but cannot remain recursively stable.

---

# RECURSION

Recursion describes state evolution where future states depend on prior structural states.

Recursive systems propagate constraints through time.

Recursion is therefore both:

- an evolution mechanism,
- and a stability challenge.

---

# RECURSIVE STATE

A recursive state is a state whose future admissibility depends on prior recursive consistency preservation.

Recursive states may stabilize, destabilize, collapse, or bifurcate.

---

# CONSTRAINT PROPAGATION

Constraint propagation describes how admissibility conditions spread through recursive systems.

Constraint propagation may occur through:

- geometry,
- topology,
- information structures,
- optimization systems,
- physical dynamics,
- or hardware-enforced execution paths.

---

# METASTABILITY

Metastability describes temporarily stable recursive structures existing near transition boundaries.

Metastable systems may:

- stabilize,
- collapse,
- bifurcate,
- or transition into new admissible regimes.

---

# STRUCTURAL TRANSITION

A structural transition is a recursive state change that alters admissibility geometry.

Transitions may include:

- horizon formation,
- recursive bifurcation,
- collapse dynamics,
- topology transitions,
- or alignment shifts.

---

# RECURSIVE GEOMETRY

Recursive geometry describes admissibility-preserving structural evolution in recursive state spaces.

This geometry may be:

- physical,
- informational,
- topological,
- computational,
- or abstract.

---

# INTEGRITY COLLAPSE

Integrity collapse occurs when recursive propagation violates admissibility constraints beyond stabilization capacity.

Collapse may appear as:

- divergence,
- incoherence,
- instability,
- contradiction,
- recursive corruption,
- or uncontrolled optimization drift.

---

# BOUNDED OPTIMIZATION

Bounded optimization describes optimization constrained by admissibility preservation.

Optimization without admissibility constraints is considered structurally unstable.

---

# RECURSIVE OPERATOR

A recursive operator transforms recursive states while preserving defined admissibility conditions.

Operators may:

- stabilize,
- reduce,
- constrain,
- propagate,
- or restructure recursive systems.

---

# ADMISSIBLE EVOLUTION

Admissible evolution describes recursive state development preserving structural consistency across recursive depth.

Admissible evolution is the central requirement across TIG, SIR, and SGI.

---

# X4

X4 describes a structural recursive admissibility coordinate.

X4 is not defined as a conventional spatial dimension.

X4 represents recursive structural state dependency within admissibility-preserving evolution.

Its exact formalization remains under active theoretical development.

---

# SEMANTIC STABILITY REQUIREMENT

All repositories using these primitives must preserve:

- semantic consistency,
- recursive compatibility,
- and admissibility-preserving interpretation.

Primitive redefinitions must be globally synchronized.
