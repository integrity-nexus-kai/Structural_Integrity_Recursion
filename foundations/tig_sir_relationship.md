# TIG and SIR: Relationship and Separation

Status: Canonical

---

## Overview

Topological Integrity Gravity (TIG) and Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) are closely related but serve different roles within the broader research architecture.

TIG investigates admissibility within physical gravitational systems.

SIR investigates admissibility within abstract recursive and mathematical systems.

Both frameworks share common structural motivations while remaining scientifically separated.

---

## TIG

Topological Integrity Gravity focuses on:

- horizon formation,
- admissibility transitions,
- bounded-curvature sectors,
- gravitational consistency conditions,
- physically realizable geometries,
- and structurally admissible spacetime configurations.

TIG is a physics framework.

---

## SIR

Structural Integrity Recursion focuses on:

- recursive admissibility,
- structural stabilization,
- semantic consistency,
- recursive integrity preservation,
- topology-aware recursion,
- and mathematically governed abstraction layers.

SIR is a mathematical framework.

---

## Historical Origin

SIR emerged during the mathematical consolidation of TIG.

While analyzing admissibility structures within TIG, recurring mathematical principles appeared that were not obviously limited to gravitational systems.

These recurring structures motivated the creation of a separate mathematical research environment.

The objective was to investigate whether the observed structural principles possess a broader mathematical formulation.

---

## Complementary Roles

TIG provides the physical discovery context.

SIR provides the mathematical abstraction environment.

TIG explores admissibility in gravitational systems.

SIR explores admissibility in recursive mathematical systems.

Both frameworks are intended to support each other while remaining independently understandable.

---

## Current Research Direction

The current objective of SIR is to investigate whether recursive systems require admissibility conditions in order to maintain structural consistency under increasing complexity.

This question is studied independently of any specific physical interpretation.

---

## Long-Term Objective

The long-term goal of SIR is to develop a mathematically rigorous framework for recursive admissibility, structural integrity, and consistency-preserving abstraction.

Whether such principles ultimately possess broader applicability remains an open research question.
