# Abstract

The central hypothesis of SIR is that stable macroscopic structure does not emerge from unrestricted complexity.

Instead, persistence, coherence, organization, and long-term stability emerge only when state evolution is constrained by integrity-preserving mechanisms.

Under this interpretation, emergence is not a spontaneous phenomenon.

Emergence is a consequence of admissibility.

The purpose of SIR is to investigate the recursive principles governing this process.

# The Problem of Unconstrained Emergence

Without governing constraints, sufficiently complex systems tend toward:

- instability,
- fragmentation,
- drift,
- contradiction,
- loss of coherence,
- entropy maximization.

Such systems may generate transient structures.

They do not reliably generate persistent structures.

This observation motivates the introduction of admissibility constraints.

# Integrity as an Emergence Regulator

SIR defines Integrity as:

the preservation of recursive structural consistency across state transitions.

Integrity does not generate structure.

Integrity constrains the space of allowable structures.

Its role is therefore regulatory rather than constructive.
