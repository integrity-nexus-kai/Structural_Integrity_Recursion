# SIR Governance Framework

## Purpose

Structural Integrity Recursion (SIR) functions as the mathematical abstraction, recursive admissibility, and semantic governance layer of the Integrity Nexus research architecture.

The purpose of this governance framework is to preserve:

- semantic consistency,
- recursive stability,
- admissibility coherence,
- publication-grade structure,
- and controlled theoretical evolution.

SIR is not treated as an uncontrolled theory-generation environment.

SIR operates as a structured consolidation and stabilization framework for recursive mathematical development.

---

# Architectural Position

The Integrity Nexus architecture is separated into layered domains:

- TIG → physical admissibility layer
- SIR → mathematical abstraction and recursive admissibility layer
- SGI → technical enforcement and implementation layer

This separation preserves structural clarity between:

- physical interpretation,
- mathematical abstraction,
- and implementation-oriented architectures.

---

# Governance Principles

## 1. Semantic Stability

All concepts must maintain stable semantic definitions across repositories.

Primitive drift, terminology inflation, and recursive ambiguity must be minimized.

---

## 2. Controlled Recursive Evolution

Recursive structures may evolve only through mathematically governed consolidation.

Uncontrolled recursive expansion is not considered admissible.

---

## 3. Admissibility Priority

Admissibility has priority over aesthetic formalism, speculative expansion, or conceptual novelty.

Mathematical consistency is treated as structurally superior to interpretational speculation.

---

## 4. Canonical Separation

All structures must be explicitly classified as:

- canonical
- exploratory
- speculative
- deprecated

Unclassified structures are considered unstable and non-canonical.

---

## 5. Publication Integrity

Publication-grade material must satisfy:

- semantic consistency,
- mathematical traceability,
- recursive stability,
- repository consistency,
- and governance classification.

---

# Recursive Consolidation Policy

SIR functions as the consolidation environment for:

- recursive admissibility structures,
- primitive stabilization,
- recursive operator models,
- admissibility-flow dynamics,
- and semantic governance structures.

Only mathematically stabilized structures may migrate into canonical physical TIG formalism.

---

# Primitive Stability Policy

Primitive definitions must remain:

- semantically stable,
- recursively traceable,
- mathematically interpretable,
- and repository-consistent.

Duplicate primitive definitions should be consolidated whenever possible.

---

# Exploratory Structure Policy

Exploratory and speculative structures are permitted.

However:

- they must remain explicitly classified,
- they must not be presented as canonical mathematics,
- and they must not destabilize existing admissibility structures.

---

# X4 Governance Rule

X4 currently remains exploratory.

No speculative physical interpretation of X4 is considered canonical TIG or SIR mathematics.

---

# Repository Governance Objective

The long-term objective of SIR governance is the transformation of recursive integrity research from:

- loosely connected exploratory structures

toward:

- publication-grade recursive admissibility architecture.
