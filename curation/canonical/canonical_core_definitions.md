# Stabilized Concepts

## 1. Integrity

Integrity describes structural consistency preservation under recursive evolution.

Integrity is not treated as a moral category.

Integrity is defined as admissibility-preserving recursive consistency.

---

## 2. Admissibility

Admissibility defines whether a recursive state, transition, or operator remains structurally valid under recursive evolution.

Formally:

- admissible if structural consistency is preserved
- critical if admissibility approaches instability
- inadmissible if recursive consistency collapses

---

## 3. Recursive State

A recursive state is a state whose future evolution depends on prior recursive structural conditions.

Recursive states may:

- stabilize,
- bifurcate,
- collapse,
- or transition across admissibility boundaries.

---

## 4. Recursive Operator

A recursive operator transforms recursive states through recursive evolution.

A recursive operator is admissibility-preserving if recursive consistency remains bounded during evolution.

---

## 5. Admissible Evolution

Admissible evolution describes recursive state development preserving structural consistency across recursive depth.

Admissible evolution is considered the central stability condition across TIG, SIR, and SGI.

---

## 6. Recursive Transition Surface

A recursive transition surface defines the boundary between admissible and inadmissible recursive evolution.

Transition surfaces may produce:

- metastability,
- recursive bifurcation,
- topology transition,
- or recursive collapse.

---

## 7. Metastability

Metastability describes temporarily stable recursive structures near critical admissibility boundaries.

Metastable systems may appear stable while accumulating recursive instability.

---

## 8. Constraint Propagation

Constraint propagation describes how admissibility conditions spread through recursive systems.

Constraint propagation may occur through:

- recursive geometry,
- topology,
- information structures,
- optimization systems,
- or physical dynamics.

---

## 9. Recursive Geometry

Recursive geometry describes structural admissibility evolution within recursive state spaces.

Recursive geometry may be:

- physical,
- informational,
- topological,
- computational,
- or abstract.

---

## 10. Recursive Collapse

Recursive collapse occurs when recursive evolution violates admissibility conditions beyond stabilization capacity.

Collapse may appear as:

- divergence,
- recursive instability,
- integrity drift,
- structural incoherence,
- or uncontrolled recursive amplification.

---
